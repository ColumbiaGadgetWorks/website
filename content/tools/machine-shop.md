The machine shop is where the heavy iron lives. A **full-size Bridgeport vertical mill** and a **Clausing engine lathe** let members work to a thousandth of an inch in steel, aluminum, and brass: shafts, bushings, brackets, fixtures, and replacement parts for machines that stopped being sold decades ago. These are the two machines that turn "I need a part that does not exist" into a part.

**What people make:** custom motorcycle and car parts, tooling for the other machines in the shop, repairs for farm and shop equipment, precision fixtures, and the occasional battlebot chassis.

## The Bridgeport

The Bridgeport is the archetypal knee mill and the reason so many small shops can make anything at all. The head tilts and swivels, the table moves in three axes, and with the right workholding it will drill, bore, face, slot, and cut keyways. It is a forgiving machine to learn on and an unforgiving one to rush.

## The Clausing

The lathe turns round things: facing, turning to diameter, drilling on center, boring, parting, and single-point threading. Most people's first lathe project is a simple shaft or a knurled handle, and most people are surprised how quickly it becomes the machine they reach for.

## Training

Both machines require training before solo use. They can seriously hurt an untrained operator, and they are also the machines most easily damaged by a bad cut. Training is one-on-one with the zone lead or a qualified member, and covers:

- **Mill:** tramming, workholding in the vise, edge-finding, reading the DRO, speeds and feeds, climb vs. conventional cuts.
- **Lathe:** chucking, tool setup and height, facing, turning, drilling, parting, and threading.

Our machining instructor runs periodic classes on the mill and lathe (see [classes](/classes/)), and a Thursday-night demo is always available. Setup notes and tooling inventories live on the [member wiki](https://wiki.comogadget.casa/).

## Safety

Safety glasses always. **No gloves at the mill or lathe**, ever: a glove that catches takes your hand with it. No loose clothing, jewelry, or long hair. Never leave a chuck key in a chuck. If you are not sure, stop and ask.
